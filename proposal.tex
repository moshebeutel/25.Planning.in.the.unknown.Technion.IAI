\section{Introduction}
In this project, we seek to advance 
practical yet theory-driven approaches for robot motion planning in unknown environments. In this problem, the robot is dropped in an unknown (or partially known) environment, and needs to either move to a specified point, find a specific item, or map the whole environment, while relying on onboard computation and perception to gather information about the map as it moves along. 

Previous work in this area can be divided into three categories:
\begin{itemize}
    \item Frontier-based planners such as D*-lite and LPA*~\cite{KOENIG200493,koenig2002d}, rely on search methods, in the spirit of A*, to efficiently (from a computational perspective) reuse information from previous iterations to guide the robot toward the target, while incorporating new information observed along the way. Specifically, those approaches assume unknown areas on the map are free and replan when the robot encounters an unexpected obstacle, through the closest known frontier point to the target. While those algorithms are widely used, they tend to behave poorly in highly cluttered environments, leading robots into dead ends. Moreover, they do not incorporate knowledge from previous environments on their own, which can hint at the structure of the current environment they are exploring. 
    \item Recent approaches rely on various machine learning techniques to improve classical planning algorithms via map prediction~\cite{wang2025cogniplan,ElhafsiIJP20,luperto2021exploration}. Specifically, the planner's decision are made based on the predicted map structure, which allows to incorportate prior experience (i.e., in the training data) to speed execution time. However, as is often the case with learning based approaches, they can underperform in out-of-distribution settings, and lack theoretical guarantees on their performance. It should be emphasized that map prediction is particularly challenging due to the high dimensionality of the task, which can produce poor results, especially with low training data, small networks, or large maps. 
    \item An often ignored line of work, considers a family of simplistic so-called "bug algorithms"~\cite{4389264, kamon1998tangentbug} wherein the robot executes basic strategies (e.g., wall following) to arrive to a goal location with miniamlistic capabilities in terms of sensors, computation, and memory. Importantly, the CBUG algorithm, and its generalizations~\cite{gabriely2005cbug,kramer2010multidimensional} is one of the few available algorithms that provides worst-case guarantees on the execution time, which is quadratic in the cost of the shortest optimal path (with full information). This should be contrasted with works in the previous two mentioned areas, whose worst-case performance is unbounded. E.g., the robot can overlook a short path to the target, and instead first explore an exceedingly long dead end before retracing its steps.  But, bug algorithms are not perfect either. As already mentioned, they are designed for overly simplistic robotic systems,  and so can underperform. 
\end{itemize}

In our work, we plan to leverage ideas across the three areas to develop an approach that is well motivated from a practical perspective, yet highly practical. A main ingredient in our approach will be GenAI (initially, Diffusion Models, and then advancing to LLMs and VLMs) which will be incorporated within a classical planning backbone. One direction that would be interesting to explore is leveraging those tools while using small models and limited training data (unlike map prediction). In this context, trajectory prediction, as in DiTree~\cite{hassidof2025trainonce}, can be combined with a Bug-like local decision making (traverse the next obstacle from the left or the right), while incorporating  increasing neighborhoods of exploration as in CBUG~\cite{gabriely2005cbug} to provide bounded worst-case performance. 

\vspace{5pt} 
\noindent \textbf{Specific next steps to explore:}
\begin{itemize}
    \item Generalizing bug-like algorithms to better exploit complex sensor data (LIDARs and cameras)
    \item Informing decisions of bug algorithms using diffusion models.
    \item Deriving compact yet effective models for the above task.
    \item Maintaining bounded competitiveness guarantees.  
\end{itemize}

\section{Research Directions and Plan}

The planned research requires integrating advanced algorithmic thechniqes: Map prediction, Diffusion policy and vision-language-models. A modular plan that verifies each building block is needed before integration. The plan will therefore contain 4 phases:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item Formulation and Baselines
    \item Kinodynamic aware planning (DiTree)
    \item Map prediction based planning (CogniPlan)
    \item Integrate VLA to planning
\end{enumerate}

\subsection*{Research Decision Point}
The completion of Phase 2 will represent a decision point: whether to advance toward map prediction (CogniPlan) or to develop a model capable of learning immediate actions based on current observation. It should be noted that generating maps on an edge device may pose significant computational challenges.


\subsection{Formulation and Baselines}
This phase establishes the initial mapping system, greedy decision-making heuristic, and the kinematic motion planning baseline.

\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{MDP definition} - Formulate the problem including reward function and terminal states
    \item \textbf{SLAM Baseline} - Use a known, trusted SLAM method (e.g. ORB SLAM 3 \cite{campos2021orbslam3}) to build a 3D map of an unknown indoor area using a drone equipped with an IMU and a monocular RGB camera. Control drone using random walk or manually.
    \item \textbf{Frontier and Goal Candidate Identification Baseline} - Continuously identify frontier and extract a set of candidate locations for potential robot movement.
    \item  \textbf{Classical Utility Function Baseline} - Define and implement a standard greedy utility function that selects the next best location by balancing distance utility and information gain utility based only on the observed map.
    \item \textbf{Classical Motion Planning Baseline} - Implement a standard Sampling-Based Planner (e.g. RRT) to execute the chosen waypoint, ensuring adherence to Kinodynamic Motion Planning (KMP) constraints through forward action propagation.
    Optional integrations:
    \begin{itemize}
        \item Effective Sampling for Robot Motion Planning Through the Lens of Lattices~\cite{PanasoffI-RSS-25}
        \item VAMP~\cite{thomason_motions_microseconds_2024}
    \end{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Bug Agents} - Small "atomic" actions programmed and verified to achieve simple immediate goals (e.g. wall following, enter a room etc.)
\end{itemize}


\subsection{DiTree Motion Planning}
This phase integrates Diffusion Policy GenAI (DiTree approach) to accelerate the low-level, physically constrained motion planning, independent of the mission planning graph from Phase 2.

\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{DiTree Integration} - Integrate the DiTree framework to guide the motion planner backbone.
    \item \textbf{Informed Action Sampling} -  Replace the motion planner's uniform random action selection with a Diffusion Policy that serves as an informed action sampler, generating action sequences conditioned on the planning context.
    \item \textbf{Generalization} - Generalize to Out-of-Distribution scenarios.
\end{itemize}


\subsection{CogniPlan Mission Planning}
This phase develops the GenAI component for map prediction to achieve non-myopic exploration, utilizing the CogniPlan approach without relying on external language commands.

\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Layout Reconstruction and Conditional Prediction} - Implement a method to reconstruct the abstract geometric layout from the observed map, modeling rooms as polygons and filtering noise. Integrate a Conditional Generative Inpainting Network (e.g. WGAN) trained to predict the unobserved map layout.
    \item \textbf{Uncertainty Modeling} - Generate a set of multiple plausible layout predictions by varying conditional input vectors, explicitly capturing layout uncertainty. Quantify this uncertainty by calculating the average of the multiple binary predictions, which represents the average predicted probability of free areas.
    \item \textbf{Informed Information Gain} - Refine the estimate of expected new area visible from a candidate location by projecting predicted walls from the layout onto the map. This provides a non optimistic estimate of information gain by treating predicted walls as obstacles.
    \item  \textbf{Graph Attention Planner} - Develop a Graph Attention Network policy that utilizes an augmented graph representation. Augment graph nodes with the average predicted probability of free areas from the averaged predictions, allowing the planner to reason over global foresight and multiple layout hypotheses.

\end{itemize}

\subsection{Unified VLA Policy and System Evaluation}
The final phase introduces the Vision-Language-Action framework for unified decision-making and conducts comprehensive system evaluation.

\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{VLA Policy Activation} - Introduce the VLA Policy Network. Encode the natural language mission goal (e.g., "Explore and map all rooms in current room") along with visual inputs, into Prefix Tokens to establish the semantic context.
    \item \textbf{Information Fusion} - VLA policy selects global goal using CogniPlan map predictions and pass it to DiTree that plans Kinodynamic  
    \item \textbf{Mission  Metrics Evaluation} - Sweep weights of plan method including the use of bug agents locally.
\end{itemize}
